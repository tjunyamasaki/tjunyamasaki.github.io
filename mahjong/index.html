A MOMENT TO LEARN

The art of riichi.

Japanese four-player mahjong. Build a winning hand from the tiles you draw, and read what everyone leaves behind.

Four groups and a pair. A group is three consecutive numbers in one suit, or three identical tiles. A pair is two identical tiles. Seven pairs and thirteen orphans are special winning shapes.

Your turn

Draw automatically, select a tile, then press Discard. A gold edge marks your newest tile. The optional suggestion considers your hand and the public table.

You need at least one yaku — a scoring pattern — to win. Dora add value but are not a yaku. A complete-looking hand may therefore be unable to win.

Riichi, tsumo, and ron

Riichi: with a closed hand one tile from winning, stake 1,000 points and choose a highlighted discard. Your hand becomes locked. Tsumo: win on your own draw. Ron: win on another player's discard.

Furiten: you cannot ron if your own discard history contains any current winning tile. Passing a winning discard also blocks ron temporarily, or for the rest of the hand after riichi. You may still tsumo.

Chi, pon, and kan

Chi claims a sequence from the player on your left. Pon claims a triplet from anyone. Kan makes four of a kind and draws a replacement tile. Calls appear only when legal; you can always pass.

Calling generally opens your hand and removes some yaku. All simples (2–8 only), a triplet of dragons, or your seat/round wind are useful open-hand patterns.

Tiles and dora

Characters, circles, and bamboo run from 1 to 9. Honors are the four winds and three dragons. Honor tiles cannot form sequences.

The tile after an indicator is dora: 9 wraps to 1; winds cycle East → South → West → North; dragons cycle white → green → red. Red fives are also dora. Ura dora are revealed for a winning riichi hand.

The table rules

25,000 starting points, three red fives, open tanyao, ippatsu, ura and kan dora. Scoring uses Japanese han/fu, including yakuman. Double ron is allowed; triple ron is an abortive draw. Dealer repeats on a win or tenpai draw.

Choose one hand, East, or East + South. East and South matches have no extra wind extension. Bankruptcy and the leading dealer's final-hand stop apply. Exhaustive draws settle tenpai payments.

Solo has no turn timer. Online decisions allow 60 seconds; inactive players pass calls or discard a legal tile. Disconnected guests are replaced by bots. The host must keep the table open.
