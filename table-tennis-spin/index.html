FEEL THE PHYSICS

A ball. Infinite possibilities.

Physics are simplified for visualization and learning.

A 40 mm, 2.7 g hollow ball. Gravity, drag, Magnus lift and friction share the same world: X right, Y up, Z down the table away from you. Topspin is defined for the ball’s travel direction. Clockwise sidespin is viewed from above.

The rotation axis is the angular velocity vector. Components add to one vector; they never alternate. Surface markers are slowed by default and the ball is enlarged in the arena. RPM and motion always use the actual physics.

Table friction acts on the bottom contact point. Pure vertical sidespin does not directly make that point slide sideways. A tilted axis can; sidespin also changes the incoming flight through Magnus lift.

Toss height is measured from the contact height to the apex. The ideal vertical toss uses gravity without air drag: v = √(2gΔh) on the descent. A higher toss does not create spin by itself.

The racket is a moving plane tangent to the chosen patch. Moving the patch also changes the face normal. Elastic rubber deformation, dwell time and net cloth motion are not modeled. Presets illustrate contact ideas, not exact professional technique or serve legality.

Flight and contact are recorded at 120 steps per second. Scrubbing replays the same result. Compare synchronizes racket impacts; a shorter toss waits before release.
